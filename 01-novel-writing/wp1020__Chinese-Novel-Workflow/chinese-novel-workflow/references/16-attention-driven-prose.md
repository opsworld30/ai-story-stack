# Attention-Driven Prose

Use this when drafting or revising prose that feels too structured, too outline-like, too evenly paragraphed, too logical, too clean, or too AI-formatted.

## Core Rule

大纲负责秩序，正文负责像人。

Do not write prose by following the visible order of outline fields. Use the POV character's live attention, sensory interruption, emotional delay, misreading, recognition, and pressured choice to decide sentence and paragraph order.

Scene cards are hidden scaffolding, not paragraph templates.

## Attention Chain

Before drafting a scene, define:

```markdown
- POV character:
- First attention point:
- Why this detail comes first:
- Interruption:
- Body reaction:
- Misread / uncertainty:
- Recognition detail:
- Pressured choice:
- Delayed emotion:
- What should remain unsaid:
- Paragraph rhythm:
```

Use the chain to enter the scene. Do not begin by explaining the full situation if the character would first notice light, pain, sound, a hand, a line of dialogue, an object, or a wrong detail.

## Action Chain Integrity

Short sentences may break rhythm; the action chain must not break.

Every visible action or close-up gesture should do at least one job:

- Carry external pressure into the body.
- Reveal or conceal the character's state.
- Prepare the next action or refusal.
- Show hesitation, restraint, misread, or tactical delay.
- Trigger another character's reaction.
- Change the scene's next beat.

Watch especially for floating gestures such as "手指停了一下", "眼神动了动", "呼吸一滞", "脚步顿住", or "指尖收紧". If the reader cannot tell why the gesture happens or what it changes, either attach it to a concrete object/impulse/consequence or delete it.

## Hidden Structure, Visible Flow

Do not expose scene-card order in prose:

```text
location -> cast -> pressure -> goal -> obstacle -> action -> reveal -> payoff
```

Instead, choose a lived sequence:

```text
sensory entry -> interruption -> body reaction -> recognition -> action
```

```text
dialogue entry -> misread -> object detail -> decision
```

```text
body pain -> sound -> memory flash -> tactical choice
```

```text
object detail -> relationship tension -> withheld emotion -> external pressure
```

## Paragraph De-Formatting

- Do not make every paragraph carry action + psychology + explanation.
- Allow one-line paragraphs for shock, recognition, refusal, dread, delayed emotion, or a hard choice.
- Allow a paragraph to contain only an action, object response, line of dialogue, or unfinished thought.
- Let some actions stand without explaining their meaning.
- Let some dialogue land without narration summarizing the emotion.
- Break paragraph symmetry when the prose starts to feel too balanced.

## Uneven Rhythm

Let pressure decide rhythm:

| Scene pressure | Rhythm direction |
| --- | --- |
| Danger, pain, panic | Short lines, broken perception, delayed explanation. |
| Tactical observation | Concrete detail first, judgment second. |
| Emotional restraint | Action and silence before direct psychology. |
| Confrontation | Dialogue, interruption, object handling, reaction beats. |
| Aftermath | Slower rhythm, but avoid summary lectures. |

## Human Roughness

Natural prose can keep useful roughness:

- Preserve small spoken turns, dry humor, repeated words, or incomplete thoughts when they fit character voice.
- Do not polish every sentence into the same smooth rhythm.
- Do not make every paragraph beautiful; make key moments precise.
- Do not explain every emotional transition.
- Let body reaction sometimes arrive before conscious thought.

## Clean But Soulless Prose

Humanizer-zh's warning about "clean but soulless" prose is especially important in fiction. A passage can have no obvious AI words and still feel machine-made.

Watch for:

- Every sentence is grammatical, complete, and similarly weighted.
- Every paragraph has a tidy action -> thought -> explanation shape.
- The POV character observes without attitude, desire, fear, irritation, humor, or bias.
- The prose reports events neutrally instead of letting the character misread, resist, delay, or choose.
- All emotions are named cleanly; none leak through silence, bad timing, object handling, or unfinished speech.
- The paragraph ends keep landing as little summaries.

Repair by returning to live attention:

- Add one specific bias from the POV character: what they care about, avoid, dislike, misread, or refuse to admit.
- Let pressure interrupt the neat sentence pattern.
- Keep a useful rough edge: a half sentence, a withheld answer, a dry thought, a repeated word, or a physical detail that changes the next action.
- Delete any "human" detail that is only decorative and does not affect pressure, choice, relationship, or attention.

## Revision Method

When prose reads like outline execution:

1. Mark the fixed facts that must remain.
2. Identify the POV character's first attention point.
3. Move explanation after perception, interruption, or action.
4. Replace field-order paragraphs with attention-chain paragraphs.
5. Break even paragraph length where pressure spikes.
6. Delete meaning summaries that the scene already shows.
7. Repair floating gestures by giving each one pressure, object, impulse, consequence, or follow-up choice.
8. Keep the original plot movement, information release, and ending pull.

## Self-Check

Before delivering prose, check:

- Did the scene enter through POV attention rather than outline field order?
- Is there a clear chain of noticing, interruption, body reaction, recognition, and choice?
- Do close-up gestures have function, consequence, or a clear link to the next choice?
- Do short standalone sentences still connect through pressure, attention, or action?
- Are paragraph lengths uneven in a purposeful way?
- Does any paragraph explain what the scene already showed?
- Is there enough delay, misread, silence, interruption, or unfinished thought?
- Does the prose have character-specific bias or roughness, rather than neutral, clean reporting?
- Did the prose keep story structure hidden while preserving plot order?
- Does the prose still obey character stage, continuity, and red lines?
